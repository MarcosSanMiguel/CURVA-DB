About CURVA

Civil Ultras Research & Visual Archive

What is an ultra? A fiercely devoted fan of a football club? Yes.

A hooligan? Sometimes. A volunteer, an organizer, a force in civil society? That too — many times all at once.

The Archive

The Civil Ultras Research & Visual Archive (CURVA) is a work-in-progress project to catalog and document football ultra groups around the world that participate in civil society activities such as volunteerism, community service, educational workshops, humanitarian relief, pro-democracy work, anti-racism events, immigrant/refugee support, toy-drives for children, anti-femicide campaigns, food kitchens, support for those facing housing eviction, housing construction such as Habitat for Humanity, environmental campaigns, and much more.

The Basic Premise

The basic premise of CURVA is that soccer ultras are often pathologized and stereotyped in the media and in research as hooligans and criminals. While violence and illicit activities are a serious problem in some ultra groups and some clubs, a very large number of ultras and ultra groups participate heavily in civil society activities that strengthen community and enrich democracy. One of CURVA’s objectives is to identify, catalog, and measure the extent to which Ultra groups are participating in civil society activities.

A Wide Spectrum of Activities

Rather than viewing Ultras as malignant, transgressive, and undesirable members of the football ecosystem that should be eliminated or controlled, CURVA demonstrates that the Ultra universe comprises a wide range of ideologies and activities. There are Ultra groups that prioritize violence and illicit activities that generate large sums of money. There are also many groups that, in addition to supporting their team with great passion, prioritize non-violence and civil society activities. A third subset of Ultras are shapeshifters, that emphasize community-oriented activities while also occasionally participating in violence against their rivals or within their group.

Civil Ultras can be politically conservative or politically progressive. CURVA intentionally seeks to identify Ultra groups that participate in significant civil society activities regardless of political orientation.

Methodology & Verification

The initial and tentative CURVA database is generated through AI with a prompt based on dozens of indicators and designed to reduce speculation or hallucinations for the chatbots. These indicators span everything from a group’s size and autonomy, to its specific volunteer or community involvements, to how it externally identifies itself through tifo displays, chants, and pyrotechnics. Each measure comes with its own complexities, so while clear patterns emerge across the ultra universe, no two groups are identical. Once the information has been uploaded from our AI platforms, the database will be verified via confirmation by CURVA researchers, the enlistment of country experts, field work, and crowd-sourcing.

Institutional Foundation

CURVA is the product of a vertically integrated project (VIP) on football and community at the Georgia Institute of Technology. In Fall 2026, the VIP team is comprised of twelve undergraduates from a range of Georgia Tech majors. The team is led by Dr. Kirk Bowman, from the Sam Nunn School of International Affairs. CURVA will be formally launched in Spring 2027 at SoccerCon II.

A Living Archive

CURVA is a living, work-in-progress archive. If you have knowledge of a group or region, as a fan, member, or researcher, we welcome corrections, additions, and sources. Contact us here.
